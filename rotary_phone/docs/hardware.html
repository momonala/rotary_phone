Rotary Phone Hardware

Rotary Phone Hardware

Single-board build: an AZ-Delivery D1 Mini (ESP8266) rings the bell through a DRV8871 H-bridge, plays audio through a DFPlayer Mini, and reads the dial and hook switch. Last updated 2026-10-27.

Wiring and mounting base

  • Everything on the base is to scale (10 px = 1 mm), seen from above with the base in its printed orientation: terminal strip along the top edge, bells and ringer relay below, the notch clearing the relay. Ear and Bell boxes are not to scale.
  • Module positions are read off a test-fit photo, so treat them as ±3 mm. D1 Mini: front view, USB on the left. DFPlayer: SD slot on the left, used pins along the top edge. Buck: 24 V in at the top, 5 V out at the bottom.
  • Base outline clockwise from the top-left of the raised edge, mm: (12,0) (110,0) (110,3) (122,3) (122,86) (87,86) (87,48) (33,48) (33,86) (0,86) (0,3) (12,3). Mounting holes Ø2.3 at (7, 26.5) and (120.5, 32.4).
  • DRV8871: header order differs between breakout boards, so wire by the silkscreen labels, not by position. IN1/IN2 are drawn as built (D5 → IN1, D0 → IN2). 24 V goes to the ⊕/⊖ POWER screw terminal.
  • Tags with the same name are one connection; every GND is one common ground. A label at a wire's end names the pin it continues to.
  • The dial has four wires: two for the pulse contact and two for the off-normal contact. One wire of each pair goes to its pin, the other to GND. The hook switch is a separate contact in the cradle, not part of the dial.

Modules

DRV8871

  • IN1 ← D5, IN2 ← D0 as built. Either way round: swapping only flips which way each half of the square wave goes. 3.3 V logic is fine; no logic supply pin.
  • 24 V into the ⊕/⊖ POWER screw terminal (rated 6.5–45 V); ⊖ straight to supply −.
  • OUT1/OUT2 → bell, either way round.
  • Current limit set by the ILIM resistor: 30 kΩ on the Adafruit board ≈ 2 A. Only lower the resistor if the bell rings weakly.
  • Both inputs LOW = coast, then sleep after about 1 ms. Inputs have internal pull-downs, so the bell stays off while the D1 Mini boots.
  • Bell drive: 25 Hz square wave, 2 s on, 4 s off.

Bell

  • Original Siemens polarized ringer (Wecker), stamped "2.54": most likely made February 1954, probably from a W48. A permanent magnet holds the armature; the coil rocks it, so it needs alternating drive.
  • Single coil, label "1500 – 17000 – 0.08 CuL" (second row "DFG Bv.001"): 1500 Ω, 17,000 turns, 0.08 mm enamelled copper. Two blue wires from its tabs go straight to DRV8871 OUT1/OUT2; nothing else is needed.
  • Healthy coil reads a steady ~1.5 kΩ, measured with power off and one wire lifted from the driver. O.L means open: the fine wire has come off a tab. A reading that jumps when a blue wire is moved points at the broken end.
  • The 0.08 mm wire is thinner than a hair and dissolves in molten solder within seconds. It broke twice: once when the blue wires were soldered to the tabs (heat), once from ringing vibration after the repair. Repair with a quick touch of a tinned iron, a slack loop, the blue wire anchored to the frame, and glue over the joint.
  • Rewinding by hand isn't practical (17,000 turns). Replacement: search "W48 Weckerspule" or "Weckerspule 1500 Ohm"; any coil with the same label numbers is a drop-in.
  • 24 V drive is marginal: these ringers were built for ~60–75 V at 25 Hz, and 24 V gives at most 16 mA. A slightly binding clapper or armature stops it. If it stays weak, raise the supply to ~36 V (DRV8871 max 45 V, LM2596 buck max 40 V).
  • Debug from /status: "DC" holds 24 V one way for 2 s (armature should clunk and hold), "AC" rings one 2 s burst. Both ignore the hook. Full 24 V AC at the driver with a silent bell means the coil circuit is open.

DFPlayer Mini

  • RX ← D1 through 1 kΩ, TX → D2, 9600 baud software serial.
  • SPK2/SPK1 (pins 1 and 3 of the row with VCC, counting from the far end) → earpiece, wired direct. Neither SPK pin may touch GND, and nothing else may sit across SPK1–SPK2.
  • Earpiece is a 240 Ω telephone receiver. Before powering a new DFPlayer, measure across the SPK1/SPK2 wires with the DFPlayer unplugged: it must read about 240 Ω.
  • Two DFPlayers burnt out because the phone's own wiring shorted the earpiece wires together, so the amp drove a near-short (it read 6.2 Ω, mistaken for the earpiece). The same short made the earpiece far too quiet through 47 Ω and from DAC_R.
  • Flash a low-volume sketch before connecting a new DFPlayer, so it can't start up at full volume.
  • Firmware volume: 15 of 30 for calls (VOLUME), 25 for robot sounds (SOUND_VOLUME), 30 for the ring tone (RING_VOLUME). Raise in steps.
  • SD card: folder 01, 21 tracks. Messages 000–011, robot sounds 012–014, songs 030–033, startup tone 098, "help me" 099. The card holds the levelled copies from tools/normalize_audio.sh: calls at −36.8 LUFS, robot sounds (012–014, 098) at −10.8 LUFS. Originals in media/01 (gitignored).

Phone contacts

  • D3 dial off-normal: LOW while the dial turns.
  • D7 dial pulse: one LOW→HIGH edge per pulse; 10 pulses = 0.
  • D6 hook: LOW when the handset is lifted. Debounced 100 ms in the firmware and system_test.
  • Status LED (D4): on while the handset is lifted, and flips for each dial click.
  • Verified 2026-10-27 with hook_test and dial_test: every digit 0–9 counted exactly, no stray pulses at rest.
  • All three are plain on/off contacts read as digital inputs, not analog. Internal pull-ups (INPUT_PULLUP); no resistors.
  • Telling the dial's two pairs apart: with a multimeter on continuity, the pulse pair flickers as the dial runs back; the off-normal pair changes state as soon as the dial leaves rest and stays that way until it returns.

Power

As built

24 V supply
 ├─ DRV8871 VM
 └─ buck IN+ (LM2596, set to 5 V)
     └─ buck OUT+
         ├─ D1 Mini 5V
         └─ DFPlayer VCC
All grounds common. No extra capacitors or diodes.

Constraints

  • 24 V, not 12 V: the bell didn't ring at 12 V.
  • Only 5 V on the D1 Mini's 5V pin.
  • DRV8871 GND goes straight to supply −, not through the D1 Mini's G pin, so bell current stays off the ESP's ground.
  • If ringing ever causes resets: add 1000 µF (35 V+) across DRV8871 VM and GND.

Build and flash

Toolchain

  • arduino-cli (Homebrew), board package esp8266:esp8266 3.1.2, library DFPlayerMini_Fast 1.2.4.
  • Board esp8266:esp8266:d1_mini. The port name changes with the USB socket (seen -110 and -310); find it with ls /dev/cu.usbserial*.
cd ~/code/projects/rotary_phone
# main firmware (WiFi from Keychain), or pass a test sketch folder
rotary_phone/tools/flash.sh
rotary_phone/tools/flash.sh system_test

# reset the board and print 20 s of serial (9600 baud)
python3 -I rotary_phone/tools/reset_and_read.py \
  ~/Library/Arduino15/packages/esp8266/hardware/esp8266/3.1.2/tools/pyserial \
  /dev/cu.usbserial-310 20

The script uses the pyserial bundled with the board package. Garbage at the start of a boot is the boot ROM at 74880 baud.

Sketches

  • system_test/: full call without WiFi. Rings until pickup, plays 099, reads the dialed number, plays its track, rings again 10 s after hang-up.
  • bell_test/: rings forever, ignores the hook.
  • audio_test/: loops tracks 098 and 030–033 at volume 30; prints volume, track count, play status.
  • hook_test/: LED on while lifted; prints every hook change.
  • dial_test/: LED on while the dial is off rest; prints each digit and any stray pulse. Debounces both contacts 5 ms.
  • rotary_phone/: main firmware, subscribed to home/rotary_phone. A track number rings, then plays that track 1 s after pickup; play:N plays track N straight away with no ring; stop silences the bell and any audio; anything else rings, plays 099 and plays the track for the dialed number. Ringing gives up after 2 min. It reports back on home/rotary_phone/status (ringing, picked_up, playing:N, dialed:N, hung_up, …), publishes a retained health snapshot on /state every 30 s and on hook changes, and keeps /availability at online, with an MQTT last will of offline. The heartbeat also re-applies the volume if the DFPlayer drifted from it. The web app shows these as toasts and on /status. WiFi comes from Keychain (rotary_phone_wifi_ssid, rotary_phone_wifi_pass) as build flags via tools/flash.sh (sketch name as the argument, default rotary_phone). dialer.ino has no pulse debounce yet; reuse dial_test's if digits miscount.
